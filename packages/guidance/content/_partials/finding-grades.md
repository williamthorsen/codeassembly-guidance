- **Confidence** states advisability: `high` is clearly worth doing, `medium` is worth doing but arguable, `low` is not worth a ticket now. A borderline item takes the lower grade. Only an item graded `high` or `medium` gets a ticket.
- **Value** states how much the item matters: `must-have`, `should-have`, `nice-to-have`, `questionable`.

An item from a code-health assessment, whose findings artifact names `assess-code-health` in its frontmatter `provenance.skill` or has a filename ending in `_code-health-findings.md`, is graded by the code that the change leaves: its conformance, its clarity, and its correctness. The assessment asks how the code would be written afresh today, a state in which the one-time cost of making the change does not exist. That cost is never a ground to lower either grade or to drop the item, whether it is importers or call sites to rewrite, files to move, or imports churned. A small side benefit, such as the little runtime that the change saves, is not a ground either: The item is graded by the code that it leaves. A lasting cost of the end state stays a ground, such as a behavior change that a user sees or the reversal of a design decision that the repository records. Sequencing against in-flight work is a blocker on the item's ticket, not a lower grade. A proposal's `size` is not a cost either: `code-health-assessor` states what it records.
